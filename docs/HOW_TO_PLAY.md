# How to Play WHODUNIT?!

Somebody's been murdered, the suspects are all lying about *something*, and the inspector won't lift a finger until you hand him a case that holds up. Grab your hat, detective.

---

## Quick start

1. **Read the case file.** It says who died, where they were found, when, and how.
2. **Search the rooms.** Hit **🔍 Investigate** and search each room. Clues go straight into your **📓 Notebook**.
3. **Question the suspects.** Pick a suspect and ask anything. Each one is hiding something.
4. **Show them what you found.** Use **🔍 Present evidence** to hold up a clue or a confession. A story that doesn't fit the facts will crack.
5. **Follow your leads.** The Notebook's **🧭 Leads** tab lists the open questions in the case.
6. **Accuse when the case is ready.** Name the killer, the weapon and the motive, and cite only the proof that backs it. You get **one** accusation, so make it count.

---

## The goal

Work out **who** did it, **with what**, and **why**, then prove it.

A lucky guess won't cut it. To win, your accusation must name:

- the right **murderer**,
- the right **weapon**,
- the right **motive**,
- and cite the **clues that actually prove it** (and, in some cases, a **confession** someone made to you).

---

## Investigating

**Rooms**

- Open **🔍 Investigate** to see every room in the house.
- Tap **🔎 Search**. Anything you find drops into your Notebook with a little fanfare.
- Room badges tell you where you stand: **Unsearched**, **✔ Searched**, **✨ Something new** or **🔒 Locked**.

**Locked rooms**

- Some rooms are closed to you at first. The lock message is a nudge ("come back when you know more"), so read it.
- Rooms open up as the case moves on: after you find something, hear something or question somebody. Once a room opens, it stays open.

**Hidden clues**

- Some clues aren't there to be found until you know what to look for. Search a room early and you may get a line like *"Something's tucked behind the dresser, but you can't reach it yet."*
- **Watch for ✨ Something new.** When a room you've already searched has something findable that wasn't there before, its badge changes from ✔ Searched to ✨ Something new. Go back and search it again.
- **Searching again is free.** A ✔ means there's nothing more for you in that room *right now*. Once you learn more, it may change to ✨.

**Your Notebook**

The Notebook has two tabs:

- **🔎 Evidence:** your **Clues** (each card shows what it is, what it looks like and where you found it) and your **Testimony** (confessions suspects have made to you; see *Testimony cards* below).
- **🧭 Leads:** the open questions in the case.

Once a card has caught someone out, it gets a note: *"Contradicts so-and-so's story."*

---

## Questioning suspects

Pick a suspect and talk. You can:

- tap a quick question: **📍 Whereabouts**, **💀 The victim**, or **🕵️ Ask about…** another suspect;
- **type anything** in the box (up to 500 characters);
- **🔍 Present evidence**: hold up a clue or a testimony card;
- **⚔️ Confront…**: drag another suspect into the room (see *Confrontations*).

**Tips for good questions**

- **Be specific.** "Where were you at a quarter past four?" gets you further than "Did you do it?"
- **Ask about things, places and times.** A suspect only commits to a story once you've actually asked about it.
- **Ask them about each other.** People are often happy to drop a neighbour in it.
- **Remember: they lie.** Everyone is hiding something, and not every secret is the murder.
- **Your own claims don't count as proof.** You can bluff ("The organ grinder saw you!"), but suspects judge you on what you can *show* them, not on what you say.

If a suspect "needs a moment" (thunder, a crackling telephone line), nothing was used up. Tap **AGAIN** or ask something else in a moment.

---

## Stress and breakdowns

Every suspect has a **stress meter**. It climbs when you put pressure on them, and the game (not the suspect) decides how much.

| Band | What you'll see |
|---|---|
| **Calm** | Composed, chatty, in control. |
| **Defensive** | Clipped answers, prickly, changing the subject. |
| **Nervous** | Stammering, over-explaining, tells showing. |
| **Panicking** | Rambling, sweating, close to cracking. |
| **Breakdown** | The big outburst. |

**What raises stress**

- Showing a clue that **breaks their story** (the biggest jump).
- Showing a clue or confession that **bears on their secrets**, or that's linked to them.
- Hard, pointed questioning.
- Showing the same thing twice nudges it up a little, but not much.

**What lowers it**

- Telling them you believe them ("That makes sense, thank you").
- Showing them a clue that only concerns *somebody else*: suspicion moves elsewhere and they relax.
- Questioning two *other* people face to face: the bystanders breathe a little easier.

**Breakdowns**

- Push a suspect to the top of the meter and they **break down**. It's loud and dramatic, and it happens **once per suspect**.
- A breakdown is a show, not a free confession: it doesn't spill secrets by itself. Afterwards the suspect stays rattled, though, and some suspects will only give up certain things once they're rattled enough.

**Tip:** soothing a suspect isn't wasted. A calm witness is better at telling you about *other* people.

---

## Cracking secrets

Every suspect has secrets, and the game decides when one comes out. Usually it's one of these:

- you show them **the right clue**;
- you show them **the right testimony card**;
- they're **stressed enough**.

Rules worth knowing:

- **One secret per exchange.** Nobody spills everything at once.
- **Small secrets come first.** Embarrassing ones slip out before serious ones.
- **A card cracks only its own secret.** Holding up a clue can only get the confession that clue is about. If the card also pushed their stress over the edge, that stress-driven confession waits for your **next ordinary question**, so after a big reveal, keep talking (with nothing on the table).
- **Some secrets come in order.** A suspect may need to admit the small thing before they'll admit the bigger one.
- **The killer never simply confesses to the murder** before you accuse. You'll catch them lying about other things, but the last step is yours.

---

## Testimony cards

When a suspect admits a secret, it becomes a **testimony card** in your Notebook: a short summary of what they told you.

- **Present it to anyone**, just like a clue: *"So-and-so has told me this… What do you say to that?"*
- **Gossip doesn't travel by itself.** A suspect only knows what someone else admitted if *you* show them the card.
- **Use it to break alibis.** If the pastry chef admits she slipped out of the bakery at four, the ventriloquist who swore "we were in the bakery together all afternoon" now has a problem. Show *him* her card.
- Some cases ask you to **cite a confession** when you accuse. If so, the accusation form will say so.

---

## Confrontations

Tap **⚔️ Confront…** to bring two suspects face to face.

- Each exchange, you question **one** of them (pick whose turn it is) and the other **reacts** in front of them.
- If the person you're questioning has already confessed something that undercuts the other's story, they'll **throw it in their face**, and that counts as the other person hearing it.
- You can **📓 Present** clues and cards during a confrontation too.
- Each exchange counts as questioning **only the one you addressed** (that is what moves their *"questioned"* badge). The other is just reacting.

**Limits**

- Each pair gets at most **6 exchanges**. Once a pair is done, they won't face off again.
- The whole game allows **12 confrontation exchanges** in total, shared across every pair. The counter on screen shows what's left.
- Spend them wisely: confront people whose stories *clash*, not people you haven't questioned yet.

---

## Leads

The **🧭 Leads** tab in your Notebook is your to-do list.

- A **NEW LEAD** pops up when something you found or heard raises a question. Each lead comes with a gentle hint about where to look next.
- A lead is **SOLVED** when you've found the thing that answers it. It gets struck through and stamped.
- Leads never tell you who did it. They point you at the *next* thread to pull.

**⚠ Check for contradictions**

There's a contradiction check in the Notebook. If something you're holding doesn't square with a story a suspect has told you, it'll name **the suspect and the topic**, but not which clue does it or what it means. That part's your job.

- It only knows about stories you've **actually heard**, so ask first, then check.
- After a hint it needs a few turns to recharge.
- "Nothing clashes… yet" means just that: go ask more questions or search more rooms.

---

## The accuse gate

The **⚖️ ACCUSE!** button starts out stamped **🔒 CASE NOT READY**. The inspector wants a proper investigation first.

Tap it any time to see your checklist. Depending on the case, it can ask for:

- **Clues:** enough evidence found;
- **Suspects:** enough people questioned properly (look for the *"questioned n/x"* badges on the suspect cards);
- **Cracks:** enough secrets uncovered;
- and sometimes particular **leads** solved.

Each case sets its own numbers, and the checklist shows them. It never tells you *which* clue or *who*, only how far along you are.

**An open gate isn't an answer.** It means you've done enough legwork to make a case. It doesn't mean you've got the right one.

---

## Making the accusation

The form has four parts, sometimes five:

1. **Who did it?** Pick a suspect.
2. **With what?** Pick the weapon from the possible weapons you've found. Only clues that could be the weapon appear here. In a case that doesn't pick out any, every clue you've found is on the list.
3. **Why?** Pick the motive.
4. **Your proof:** cite the clues that prove it. The form shows **"Cite up to N"**: that's what this case asks for, plus one spare.
5. **A confession** (only in some cases): cite the testimony cards that prove it, again up to the number the form shows.

Whatever the case, it's always somewhere from 1 to 5 clues and 1 to 3 confessions, so read the number on the form.

Then the game asks **"Are you sure?"** and warns you: *"This ends the case. There's no taking it back."* It means it: **one accusation per game, no take-backs.**

You can only cite clues you've found and confessions someone actually made to you.

---

## How accusations are graded

The game grades you, not a suspect, so no amount of charm changes the verdict.

**Cite only what proves your case.** Every clue and confession you cite is checked.

| You got… | Result |
|---|---|
| **Clean win:** right murderer, weapon and motive, with the proof the case needs and nothing that doesn't back your story | **CASE CLOSED!** The culprit confesses, and you get the full recap: who, how, why, where and when. Textbook police work. |
| **Sloppy win:** the same, but you padded the case with clues or confessions that don't back your story | **CASE CLOSED!** Same confession, same recap, but a lower rating. The record notes the sloppy police work with a pointed remark at the end. |
| **Right person, wrong story:** wrong weapon or motive, or proof that doesn't hold up | **The murderer escaped.** Naming the right person isn't enough if you can't prove it. |
| **Wrong accusation:** the wrong person | **The murderer escaped.** Your suspect protests, goes home, and the case goes unsolved. |

Good to know:

- **The time and place aren't graded.** You don't pick them on the form. They show up in the winning recap.
- **Padding never rescues a case.** Extra clues can't turn a loss into a win. They can only turn a clean win into a sloppy one.
- **A loss doesn't say what you got wrong.** No "so close!", no solution, no breakdown of which part missed. The case simply goes unsolved.
- **PLAY AGAIN** starts the case over from scratch.

---

## Good detective habits

- **Search everything first, then search again later.** New knowledge opens new corners.
- **Get everyone's story early.** Ask each suspect where they were and what they saw before you start throwing evidence around.
- **Write the timeline in your head.** Who was where, and when? Lies usually fall apart on times and places.
- **Present with purpose.** Show a clue to the person whose story it contradicts, not to everyone in turn.
- **Follow up after a reveal.** A big card can set up a confession that only comes out on your next plain question.
- **Share confessions.** A secret one suspect gives up is often the crowbar for someone else's alibi.
- **Not every liar is a killer.** People lie to hide debts, scandals and plain embarrassment too. Sort the *petty* secrets from the *deadly* one.
- **Watch for red herrings.** A clue that seems to point at someone might just be pointing at an embarrassing secret.
- **Proof, not hunches.** Before you accuse, ask yourself: *which clue proves the weapon? Which proves the motive? Which puts this person in the frame?*
- **A tidy evidence bag beats a full one.** If you can't say what a clue proves, leave it out.
- **Save your confrontations** for two people whose stories can't both be true.
- **Don't rush the button.** When the gate opens you're *allowed* to accuse, not *told* to.

Good luck, detective. Somebody in this house is counting on you not noticing something.
